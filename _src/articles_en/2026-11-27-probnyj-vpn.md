## Why test a VPN when there are reviews

Because a VPN in Russia behaves differently on different networks. The same service can fly on home Wi-Fi in Moscow and fail to connect on mobile data in St Petersburg. In November 2026 this is very visible: single servers and whole hosting subnets get blocked, and mobile internet is often restricted at night or switched to whitelist mode. Recent cases by region are on the <a href="/en/status/">outage status page</a>.

Reviews tell you how a service worked for other people. A trial tells you how it works for you.

## Where to get a VPN trial

| Where | How it usually works | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| The service's Telegram bot | trial days start right away, the subscription link arrives in the chat | whether a card is needed, how many devices you can add |
| An app in the App Store or Google Play | the trial is a store subscription | billing starts afterwards unless you cancel in time |
| The service's website | email sign-up, sometimes "trial" means paying first with a refund window | refund terms and deadlines |
| "Free keys" in chats and channels | one key shared by thousands | blocked fast, and you don't know who sees your traffic |

For Russia the handiest option is a Telegram bot. You paste the subscription into a free client like Happ or Incy in a couple of minutes, with no accounts or app stores. Step by step: <a href="/en/podklyuchenie/">setup guide</a>.

## Trial with or without a card

A trial without a card is safer: nothing gets charged when it ends. You just decide whether to continue.

With a card or through an app store there's a risk of auto-renewal. In the App Store a subscription renews automatically unless you turn it off at least 24 hours before the trial ends. If you start a trial through a store, set a reminder for the day before it ends.

## How to test a VPN in a 3-day trial

1. **Mobile data.** Turn off Wi-Fi and use the VPN for at least an hour at different times of day. This is where most services fail. If it won't hold on mobile data, read <a href="/en/blog/ne-rabotaet-vpn-na-mobilnom/">why that happens</a>.
2. **Home Wi-Fi and a laptop.** Connect a second device and check how many devices the plan allows.
3. **YouTube and Telegram.** Play a video in HD, send a few photos and voice messages. If the video cuts out after a minute, that's a bad sign.
4. **Whitelist mode.** If your region gets whitelists, wait for one and see whether the VPN connects. The <a href="/en/check/">connection check</a> tells you when whitelists are on. More in <a href="/en/blog/vpn-s-belymi-spiskami/">VPN that works with whitelists</a>.
5. **Support.** Ask a simple question and time the reply. When a server gets blocked, that speed matters most.
6. **Server updates.** Tap "Update" on the subscription in your app. If the server list changes, the service is keeping up with blocks.

## FAQ

### Which VPNs offer a free trial for Russia?

Many paid services do, especially those that work through a Telegram bot. Compare what you can test during the trial rather than its length: mobile data, whitelist mode, the number of devices you need. Other criteria are in <a href="/en/blog/kak-vybrat-vpn-2026/">which VPN works in Russia</a>.

### How long is a VPN trial?

Usually from one day to a week. Three days is enough to test on a weekday, in the evening and at night, on Wi-Fi and on mobile data.

### Can I extend a trial?

Usually not: a trial is given once per account. Start testing when you have time, not five minutes before a trip.

### Why is a trial better than a free VPN?

Free VPNs and public keys are overloaded and get blocked quickly. A trial gives you the same servers paying users get, so the test is fair.
