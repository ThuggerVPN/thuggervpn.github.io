<p class="lead"><b>In short:</b> when a foreign SIM connects to a Russian network, mobile data and SMS are switched off for the first 24 hours. Calls and Wi-Fi keep working. Your carrier in Russia usually sends an SMS with a link to a page where you pass a quick check (a captcha), and data comes back within minutes. If you skip it, the block lifts by itself after 24 hours. Set up a VPN and offline maps before you fly, because the first day is the hardest.</p>

## What exactly is blocked

| What | During the first 24 hours |
|---|---|
| Mobile data | blocked |
| SMS (including bank and login codes) | blocked |
| Voice calls | work |
| Wi-Fi at the airport, hotel or café | works |

The rule is called a "cooling-off period". It was proposed in August 2025 and started rolling out in the autumn of that year. According to reports, it covers any SIM card that arrives from abroad, and also SIM cards that were inactive for more than 72 hours, so it can hit a Russian SIM too if you kept it switched off during a long trip.

## Why Russia does this

Russia's Ministry of Digital Development said SIM cards with mobile internet can be fitted inside drones and used for navigation, and that the temporary block improves protection against them. Critics point out that the measure has no clear basis in federal law, and some analysts doubt drones really rely on mobile data. For a traveller the reason matters less than the result: plan your first day as if you had no data.

## How to lift the block early

1. **Wait for the SMS from the Russian network.** It contains a link to a verification page.
2. **Open the link over mobile data**, not Wi-Fi, and with your VPN turned off. The page is reachable during the block.
3. **Pass the captcha.** Mobile data normally comes back within a few minutes.
4. **No smartphone or no SMS?** Call the operator's support line. A basic phone can be verified over a call.

If none of this works, the block ends on its own 24 hours after you connected.

## Gotchas worth knowing

- **The timer can restart.** Reports say crossing into another region or switching to a different network can trigger a new 24-hour block. On a trip across several regions expect patchy data.
- **You lose SMS codes.** Banking apps and services that log you in by SMS won't work during the block. Sort out logins before you fly, or switch to app-based codes.
- **Regional shutdowns are a separate thing.** Even after the block is lifted, mobile internet in many Russian cities is switched off for hours during drone alerts, and only whitelisted Russian services work. Check the <a href="/en/status/">outage status page</a> to see where it's happening today.
- **A local SIM is not a quick fix.** Getting a Russian SIM as a foreigner involves extra paperwork, which can take more than a day.

## What to prepare before you fly

- **Install a VPN at home**, while app stores and sites still open normally. In Russia many app downloads and VPN websites are blocked. Our <a href="/en/podklyuchenie/">setup guide</a> takes about 2 minutes.
- **Download offline maps** of the cities you're visiting.
- **Save your hotel address and booking** as screenshots.
- **Tell family you might be offline** for the first day and agree on a backup way to reach you. WhatsApp is unreliable in Russia, see <a href="/en/blog/does-whatsapp-work-in-russia/">does WhatsApp work in Russia</a>.

## FAQ

### Why does my foreign SIM have no internet in Russia?

Because of the 24-hour block on mobile data and SMS for SIM cards arriving from abroad. Calls work. You can lift it early through the verification link your Russian network sends by SMS.

### Does the 24-hour block apply to Russian SIM cards?

Reports say yes, if the SIM comes back from roaming or was inactive for more than 72 hours.

### Does a VPN help during the 24-hour block?

No. The block switches off mobile data itself, so there's nothing for the VPN to connect through. On Wi-Fi a VPN works as usual. Turn it off while you open the verification link.

### Will I get the block again when I travel to another city?

Possibly. Travellers report the timer can restart when you cross into another region or switch networks. If data disappears again, look for a new SMS with the verification link.
