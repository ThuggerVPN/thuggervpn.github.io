<p class="lead"><b>In short:</b> when a Russian carrier switches on whitelist mode, a regular VPN stops connecting because its servers aren't among the allowed addresses. Only services with separate technology for this mode keep working. It costs more than a normal VPN and gets targeted regularly, so there is one rule: test it in whitelist mode during a free trial, before you pay.</p>

## Why a regular VPN doesn't work under whitelists

In whitelist mode mobile data only lets through pre-approved sites and apps: Gosuslugi, banks, VK, Yandex, marketplaces. According to the Ministry of Digital Development, about 900 resources are on the list. VPN servers aren't, so the VPN app can't connect: the icon spins and pages don't load.

Your phone isn't broken and it isn't one VPN's fault. Every ordinary service behaves like this during restrictions. How the mode works is covered in <a href="/en/blog/belye-spiski-chto-eto/">Internet whitelists in Russia</a>.

## How to tell it's whitelist mode

| Sign | Whitelist mode | VPN is broken |
|---|---|---|
| Gosuslugi and VK open without a VPN | yes | yes |
| Google and Wikipedia open without a VPN | no | yes |
| VPN works on Wi-Fi | yes | usually not |
| Friends on the same carrier have the same problem | yes | no |

The quickest check is the <a href="/en/check/">connection check</a> page: open the "Whitelists" tab, turn off the VPN and Wi-Fi, and press the button. You get the result in 5 seconds.

## Which VPN works with whitelists

Only services that keep separate servers specifically for this mode can work in it. What to know:

- **It's a separate option, not a regular VPN.** It needs special infrastructure, so it's often sold at a higher price or as an add-on.
- **These setups get shut down.** According to the Telegram channel "Kod Durova", cloud providers were barred from renting out whitelisted addresses, and many services lost their servers for this mode at once. A service that worked a month ago may not work today.
- **A promise means nothing without a test.** A decent service gives you a trial long enough to catch whitelist mode and see for yourself.
- **Stability differs between carriers.** What works on MTS may work worse on T2, and the other way round.

## How to test a VPN with whitelists before paying

1. Take a trial and set the VPN up in advance, while the internet still works. The <a href="/en/podklyuchenie/">setup guide</a> takes 2 minutes.
2. Wait until whitelist mode is switched on where you are. Fresh regional info is on the <a href="/en/status/">outage status</a> page.
3. Turn off Wi-Fi, turn on the VPN and open Telegram or YouTube.
4. Test more than once: at different times of day and, if you can, on different SIM cards.
5. Pay only if it works for you, not in someone's review.

## What to do if your VPN doesn't work under whitelists

- **Connect to Wi-Fi.** Whitelists don't affect home internet.
- **Refresh the subscription in the app.** Servers for this mode change more often than regular ones.
- **Try another location** if the service lets you choose.
- **Contact the service's support** and mention your carrier and region: it makes the cause easier to find.

## FAQ

### Is there a VPN that works with whitelists?

Yes, some services have a separate option for this mode. By its nature it isn't fully stable, so test it during a trial instead of relying on promises.

### Why does a VPN with whitelists cost more?

It needs special servers that are harder to find and keep, and they have to be replaced regularly. That's why the option is usually sold as an add-on to the main plan.

### Does a free VPN work under whitelists?

As a rule, no. Free services use ordinary servers abroad, and those addresses aren't on the whitelist.

### Do I need a VPN with whitelists on home internet?

No. Whitelists are switched on for mobile data only. At home a regular VPN is enough.
