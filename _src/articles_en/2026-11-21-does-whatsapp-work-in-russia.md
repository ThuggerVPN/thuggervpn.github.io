<p class="lead"><b>In short:</b> not reliably. Russia restricted WhatsApp calls in August 2025, and in February 2026 WhatsApp said the authorities had tried to block the app completely. Without a VPN, expect texts to arrive late or not at all, and photos, voice notes and calls to fail. Set up a VPN and a backup way to reach your family before you fly.</p>

## What happened to WhatsApp in Russia

- **August 2025:** Roskomnadzor, the Russian telecom regulator, started restricting voice and video calls in WhatsApp and Telegram. It said this was to fight fraud and terrorism.
- **11-12 February 2026:** WhatsApp domains were reportedly removed from Russia's national domain name system. On 12 February WhatsApp posted on X that Russian authorities had tried to block the app fully, and called it a step back for people's safety.
- **Kremlin position:** spokesman Dmitry Peskov said it was a matter of complying with Russian law, and that a deal was possible only if Meta entered into dialogue.

Since then, the app has worked only partly and unpredictably, depending on the city, the network and the day.

## What works and what doesn't

Based on traveller reports collected by travel blogs and eSIM sellers this year (not official data):

| Connection | Without a VPN | With a VPN |
|---|---|---|
| Russian SIM, mobile data | usually fails, sometimes text only | usually works |
| Foreign SIM or eSIM in roaming | patchy, got worse from summer 2026 | the most reliable combination |
| Hotel or café Wi-Fi | rarely works | usually works |
| Mobile data during a local shutdown | nothing works | nothing works, use Wi-Fi |

Typical symptoms: text messages get through with a delay, while voice notes, photos and videos stay stuck on loading. Calls inside WhatsApp should be treated as unavailable without a VPN. Ordinary phone calls and SMS are not affected and keep working even when mobile data is cut.

Some eSIM sellers say roaming no longer reliably bypasses Russian blocks since summer 2026, because filtering now applies to roaming traffic too. Results vary between providers, so don't count on roaming alone.

## How to stay reachable

1. **Install and test a VPN at home.** Many VPN apps are not available in the Russian App Store and Google Play, and downloading one on a restricted connection is painful. Test it on mobile data before you leave.
2. **Agree on a backup channel.** Tell family and colleagues that WhatsApp may be silent and give them a phone number they can call or text normally.
3. **Know WhatsApp's built-in proxy.** Since 2023 WhatsApp has supported proxies (Settings → Storage and data → Proxy). It can help, but only with a proxy you trust. Random public addresses from channels are risky and short-lived.
4. **Prefer Wi-Fi during shutdowns.** Many Russian regions cut mobile internet during drone alerts. Hotel and home Wi-Fi usually stays up. See the [live status page](/en/status/) for current outages.
5. **Download media in advance.** Save tickets, bookings and important photos offline instead of relying on a chat to load them when you need them.

## If you're Russian and visiting from abroad

Since November 2025, Russian operators have applied a "cooling period" to Russian SIM cards coming back from abroad: mobile data is blocked for 24 hours. Usually the operator sends an SMS with a link where you can confirm you're a person and lift it earlier. Calls work during that time. Keep a foreign eSIM or Wi-Fi handy for the first day.

## FAQ

### Is using WhatsApp or a VPN illegal for tourists in Russia?
As of 2026, Russian officials have said there is no liability for simply using a VPN, while advertising VPNs and accessing content banned as "extremist" are restricted. Rules change often. This is not legal advice.

### Does WhatsApp work in Moscow and St Petersburg?
The same restrictions apply across Russia. Big cities have better Wi-Fi, but they also see mobile internet outages. Without a VPN, expect the same patchy results as elsewhere.

### What messenger do Russians use instead?
Many still use Telegram with a VPN, though it is restricted too. The state promotes its own messenger, Max, but it reportedly accepts phone numbers from only some countries, so it's not a practical option for most visitors.

### Will my WhatsApp chats be lost?
No. Messages wait on the server and arrive once you connect through a VPN or leave Russia. Back up your chats before the trip anyway.
